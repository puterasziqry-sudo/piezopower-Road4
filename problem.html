01 / PROBLEM

The road carries energy
we do not capture.

Vehicle traffic creates repeated mechanical loading. At the same time, street lighting needs reliable electricity after sunset.

The problem

Traffic creates mechanical loading, but the energy pathway is usually unused.

Roads are used by a large number of vehicles every day, producing continuous mechanical pressure on the road surface. However, this energy is usually wasted instead of being utilised.

01

Mechanical energy is dissipated

Vehicle loading produces repeated stress and strain in pavement systems. Most of that mechanical interaction is not recovered as useful electricity.

02

Lighting needs occur later

Street lighting is primarily needed after sunset, creating a natural opportunity for an energy-storage system rather than an instant-use system.

03

Road traffic is variable

Vehicle count, axle load, speed and traffic patterns differ by road. A practical system therefore needs site-specific energy budgeting and testing.

The design challenge

How can we harvest enough energy
without compromising the road?

PiezoPower Road must work as an energy system and as road infrastructure. The harvesting layer therefore has to balance structural strength, electrical output and long-term cost.

01

Durability

Embedded modules must tolerate repeated vehicle loading, moisture, temperature changes and maintenance conditions without weakening the road surface.

02

Efficiency

Only part of the mechanical input can become usable electricity, so the system needs efficient conversion, conditioning and storage.

03

Economics

The useful energy delivered must justify installation, replacement and maintenance costs over the system's operating life.
